If you sell anything — cakes, nails, consulting, a digital course, hand-painted earrings — you have had this moment. A client asks "how much?" and your stomach drops. You blurt out a number that's lower than you meant, then spend the night annoyed at yourself.

You are not bad at business. You were just never given a method. Here is one.

## Start with three numbers, not one

Before you can name a price, you need to know three things. Most people skip straight to step three and wonder why they're broke.

1. **Your cost per unit** — every peso that goes *into* making one of the thing. Materials, packaging, the delivery, the fee the payment app takes. Be honest. Include the small stuff; the small stuff is where the leak is.
2. **Your time, valued** — pick an hourly rate you'd be content earning, then count the real hours, including the back-and-forth on WhatsApp.
3. **Your margin** — what's left to actually grow. If you sell at exactly cost plus time, you have a job, not a business.

Your price is **cost + time + margin.** Not "what the lady down the street charges." Not "what feels polite."

## The number should make you slightly uncomfortable

Here is the honest part. If your price feels totally comfortable to say, it's probably too low.

> Charging fairly is not arrogance. It's the only way the work survives long enough to get good.

A price that makes you swallow once before you say it is usually right. A price that feels easy is usually leaving money — and respect — on the table.

## Three ways to raise prices without losing everyone

Afraid that a higher price will scare clients away? Don't drop the price. Change the offer.

- **Tier it.** Keep a smaller, simpler version at the old price; add a premium option above it. Most people pick the middle — and now your middle is higher.
- **Bundle it.** Three sessions for a small discount feels generous to the client and stabilizes your income.
- **Grandfather your favorites.** Tell loyal early clients they keep the old rate for one more order. They feel special; new clients pay full price.

## A script for when your voice shakes

When the client asks the price, you do not explain, apologize, or list your costs out loud. You say the number, then you stop talking.

"It's RD$2,500." Full stop. Silence is not rejection. Let them fill it.

The women who win at this aren't the ones with the cheapest price. They're the ones who can say their number calmly and wait. Practice yours out loud before the next client asks.
